0000: See like a model (no math yet)

Win: in 15 minutes you can point at any photo and say "that's a box, that's hidden, that's cut, that's good overlap". That's all lesson 0001 assumed. We use PPE photos throughout — PPE = Personal Protective Equipment (helmets, safety vests).

1. A box = sticky note around the thing

Think getBoundingClientRect() — smallest rectangle that hugs the visible pixels. No margin, no guessing hidden parts.

Tight = touches head top, feet bottom, shoulders sides. Loose = adds air → model learns air = bad.

2. Occluded = hidden behind something

Person behind a pillar. You still see half of them, so you still label — but mark occluded=yes.

Analogy: overflow:hidden — element exists, part is covered. Rule we use later: if you see >50%, label it.

3. Truncated = cut by photo edge

Person walks half out of frame. Box only covers what's in the photo, mark truncated=yes.

Analogy: viewport clipping — you don't draw outside the screen.

4. IoU — Intersection over Union

Stands for: Intersection (overlap, the AND — pixels in both boxes) over Union (total, the OR — pixels in at least one box). Same idea as Set.intersection / Set.union in code. Also called Jaccard index.

Why called that: because you literally compute intersection ÷ union. Two people draw a box around the same helmet — how similar? Overlap ÷ total area.

  • Almost same box → IoU ~0.85 → great, you agree.
  • Half overlapping → IoU ~0.4 → someone was loose.
  • Barely touching → IoU ~0.1 → different objects.
Why we use it (not pixel distance): 20px error is huge for a helmet, nothing for a truck. IoU is always 0-to-1, independent of size, and punishes shifted + too-big + too-small in one number. That's why it decides both “do annotators agree?” and later “does a model prediction count as correct?”.
Worked example: two 10×10 boxes overlapping 5×5 → 25 ÷ 175 ≈ 0.14 → no match.
• Intersection = 5×5 = 25 (only the overlap).
• Union = 100 + 100 − 25 = 175. Why −25? You counted the overlap twice (once inside each 100), so subtract one copy. Not 100 (that's just one box), not 200 (that double-counts overlap), not 150. Union = everything covered by at least one box.
Code: src/metrics.py. Rule: >0.7 = friends, <0.5 = fix guidelines.

5. mAP — mean Average Precision (mAP50 = at IoU ≥ 0.5)

Stands for, inside-out:
• Precision = correct predictions ÷ all predictions (“when it shouts helmet, how often right?”). Recall = found objects ÷ all real objects (“of all real helmets, how many found?”).
• Average Precision (AP) = average of precision across recall levels — area under the precision-recall curve, one number per class. Needed because you can cheat with thresholds.
• mean = average AP across classes, so person (1000x) doesn’t drown helmet (50x).
• 50 = a prediction counts as correct if its IoU with truth ≥ 0.5.

Why called that: inherited from search engines + COCO challenge — AP summarizes the precision-vs-recall tradeoff, mean makes it fair across classes.
Why we use it: one report-card grade to compare models. If IoU is the “ruler for one box”, mAP is the “final grade for the whole model”. You don’t need it until lesson 0005 — until then just read it as “grade”.
