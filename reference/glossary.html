Glossary

Rule for this glossary: every entry gives Stands for → Why called that → Why used, even for “common” words. Compressed definitions. Lessons link here.

PPE — Personal Protective Equipment
Stands for: gear worn to prevent injury: helmets (hard hats), high-vis vests, gloves, boots, goggles. Why called that: legal/safety term — “equipment” that “protects” the “person”. Why used here: our demo domain is construction-site safety: detect if workers wear helmet + vest. Concrete, visual, meaningful.
Ontology
Stands for: from philosophy, what exists. Why called that: you declare what exists in your world. Why used: fixed classes + attributes stop label sprawl (“hardhat” vs “helmet”).
Annotation guideline
Stands for: rules + pictures for edge cases. Why used: resolves occlusion, truncation, tiny boxes, tightness before they kill agreement.
Bounding box (bbox)
Stands for: [x_min, y_min, width, height] in pixels (COCO style). Why called that: bounds the object. Why used: tight = touches outermost pixels; “tight” matters because loose air drops IoU.
IoU — Intersection over Union
Stands for: Intersection (overlap area, the AND) over Union (total area covered by both, the OR). Borrowed from set theory / Venn diagrams, also called Jaccard index.
Why called that: literally Intersection ÷ Union. Why used: single 0-to-1 score that punishes shifted, too-big, and too-small boxes equally, independent of object size. >0.5 match, >0.75 good.
COCO — Common Objects in Context
Stands for: dataset + JSON with images, annotations, categories. Why called that: objects photographed in everyday context. Why used: lingua franca CVAT exports, training reads.
CVAT — Computer Vision Annotation Tool / Project / Task / Job
Stands for: Intel/OpenCV labeling tool. Project = ontology + tasks. Task = data chunk. Job = annotator slice. Why used: splits work, handles images + video tracks.
YOLO — You Only Look Once
Stands for: detector that predicts all boxes in one pass (not sliding windows). Why called that: single forward pass. Why used: fast, CPU-friendly (nano = 6M params), ideal for local pre-labeling.
Track / tracklet
Stands for: same object ID across video frames. Why called that: you “track” it through time. Why used: video needs identity, not just per-frame boxes; CVAT interpolates between keyframes.
ID switch
Stands for: tracker swaps IDs between two people. Why used: core video error metric — counts identity breaks.
Inter-annotator agreement
Stands for: mean IoU + class agreement between two labelers. Why used: <0.7 mean IoU = fix guidelines, don’t blame people.
Transfer learning
Stands for: start from yolov8n.pt pre-trained on COCO, fine-tune on yours. Why called that: transfers general vision knowledge. Why used: 300 images enough because model already knows edges, people.
Pre-label / model-in-the-loop
Stands for: model drafts, human corrects in same UI. Why used: cuts time/img; measure correction % to know if helping.
mAP — mean Average Precision (mAP50 = at IoU≥0.5)
Stands for: mean (averaged over classes) Average Precision (area under the precision-recall curve per class). Precision = correct predictions ÷ all predictions; Recall = found objects ÷ all real objects.
Why called that: AP summarizes the precision-vs-recall tradeoff into one number per class, then mean averages across classes so rare classes count equally.
Why used: one report-card number to compare detectors. Different from human IoU agreement — model grade vs human agreement.

Sources: CVAT docs, COCO, Ultralytics
