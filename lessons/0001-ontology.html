0001: Define a 3-class ontology that survives edge cases

Win: in 20 minutes you will write an ontology for PPE — PPE stands for Personal Protective Equipment (helmets, vests, boots — gear that protects the person; called that in safety law) — that answers 5 nasty edge cases. This is the #1 hiring signal in annotation.

Knowledge (5 min): jargon first
• Ontology — stands for: from philosophy, study of “what exists”. In annotation: fixed list of what you’re allowed to label. Why called that: you declare what exists in your world. Why used: stops everyone inventing labels (“hardhat” vs “helmet”).
• Attribute — stands for: extra flag on a label, e.g. occluded=yes. Why called that: Latin “attribuere”, something assigned to a thing. Why used: keep class list small (3 classes) but still record nuance.
• Guideline — stands for: written rules + pictures for edge cases. Why called that: guides judgment where rules are fuzzy. Why used: if two reasonable people disagree, your guideline is broken, not the people. Every vague class costs ~10% agreement.
• Edge case — stands for: rare but real situation at the “edge” of your rules (helmet in hand, reflection). Why used: models fail on edges, so you must decide them upfront.
See CVAT Projects: labels.

Your ontology (copy to annotation-guidelines.md)

person | helmet | safety-vest
attributes: occluded=[yes/no], truncated=[yes/no]

Decide these 5 now (write 1 line each)

1. Helmet in hand vs on head? → Only on-head counts. Why: downstream safety model cares about worn.
2. Person < 30px tall (“px” = pixels, tiny dots)? → Skip. Why: too few pixels = noise, hurts model grade (mAP — mean Average Precision, see 0000 §5).
3. Vest half-visible behind pillar? → Label if >50% visible, mark occluded=yes.
4. Box tightness (“tight” = touches outermost visible pixels, no air margin)? → Touch outermost visible pixels. Loose boxes drop IoU (Intersection over Union, overlap ÷ total, see 0000 §4) by ~0.15.
5. Reflection of person in window? → Never label reflections.
